Content Strategy · Hindi · 2026

100-Video Hindi Series Plan
Career Skills × AI × ForkScape

One episode per career from the guide, in Hindi, for Indian job seekers and upskillers — with an honest assessment of whether this is worth a year of your life, and the production system that makes it survivable.

The Verdict First

The idea is good — the default execution of it is not. "100 videos, one per skill" as narrated listicles will die in the algorithm: that exact content already exists in Hindi at scale, and AI-generated versions of it are flooding in. But you own one thing nobody else has: every one of your 100 careers ships with a 7-day experiment. If the series format is "मैंने 7 दिन में ये try किया" — you actually running the experiment on camera each week — that is proof-of-work content AI cannot fake and competitors won't grind out. Do that, and 100 episodes is not a content calendar, it's a moat.

The sustainability math

100 episodes = ~50 weeks at 2/week. Most channels die at episode 15. That is why this plan is phased with kill/continue gates, not a blind 100-video vow.

Positioning — the one sentence

"हर हफ्ते एक नया करियर — 7 दिन, ₹0 बजट, असली एक्सपेरिमेंट। AI मेरा साथी, नतीजा कैमरे पर।"

Every episode answers the same five questions in the same order, so the audience knows exactly what they're getting: (1) यह काम असल में क्या है, (2) 2026 में कमाई कितनी — असली आंकड़े, (3) मेरा 7-दिन का एक्सपेरिमेंट, (4) free में कहाँ से सीखें, (5) AI + ForkScape से पहला कदम आज कैसे लें.

Format System — every episode ships as a bundle

1 × Long-form (8–12 min, Hinglish)

The experiment narrative: goal on day 1 → attempts → what broke → what a real practitioner said → the money math → the free-course path. Chapters + English keywords in title/tags for the CPM bump.

3 × Shorts per episode

Cut from the long-form: the result reveal, the salary/कमाई stat, the "पहला कदम आज" clip. Shorts link to the long-form and the guide PDF.

1 × Community post + PDF hook

Each episode gives away the matching page of your Gujarati/English career guide PDF — the lead magnet you already built. Comments question of the week feeds the next episode's intro.

1 × ForkScape segment (60–90 sec, every episode)

A recurring on-screen segment: the episode's research tree live in ForkScape — one root node per career, branches for salary research / expert questions / the 7-day plan. Same segment music, same layout, every week. See integration rules below.

ForkScape Integration — useful, not an ad

  1. Make it the visible workflow, not a sponsor read. The audience watches you fork a branch for "कमाई के असली आंकड़े", another for "7-दिन plan", another for the rabbit-hole questions. The tool is the how-I-work, the way editors show their timeline. That converts; ad reads don't.
  2. One reusable canvas per episode → a downloadable template. Export/share the episode's learning tree so viewers start from your structure. "Description में canvas का link" is a subscribe driver and a direct download funnel.
  3. The builder episodes are the showcase. In episodes 33 (Web Developer), 37 (AI/ML), 38 (AI Automation), 93 (No-Code) — let ForkScape's agent orchestration build the project skeleton on camera, then you modify it live. That's the product demo disguised as education.
  4. Disclose it. It's your product, so say so — "ForkScape हमारा अपना app है, free है" — every time. ASCI's influencer guidelines require disclosure of material connection, and honesty here builds the channel's core asset: trust.
  5. Measure it. UTM-tagged download links per episode. If 100 episodes don't move downloads, the segment gets redesigned — the channel is also a product funnel, and it should be judged like one.

The Phased Plan — with kill/continue gates

Phase 1 · Episodes 1–10 (validation)

The 10 highest-search careers (marked green below). 10 weeks. Gate: if by episode 10 you have no video over 10k views and CTR under 4%, change format before making more — do not push to 100 on momentum.

Phase 2 · Episodes 11–40

The 30 next-highest-demand careers. Double down on whatever Phase 1 proved: the top 20% of episodes tell you which categories your audience actually is (screen workers? trades? business?).

Phase 3 · Episodes 41–100

Complete the set — the completeness itself becomes the brand ("YouTube का सबसे बड़ा career encyclopedia, हिंदी में"). Weaker-demand careers ride playlist traffic from strong ones in the same category.

Production Pipeline — one episode in ~10 hours

  1. Research (2h, AI-assisted): ForkScape canvas per episode — pull the career's row from your guide, verify salary claims against Naukri/BLS-equivalent sources, draft the 7-day plan. AI drafts; you verify every number that goes on screen.
  2. The experiment (spread over 7 days, ~3h of it filmed): phone footage is fine; the format's honesty is the production value. Log daily 60-second updates — those become the Shorts.
  3. Script + shoot the frame (2h): intro/outro and the five-question structure to camera. Hinglish, tight, no lecture.
  4. Edit (3h, or outsource at ₹1,500–3,000/ep): by episode 20 this must not be you — it's the first hire, paid for by the first sponsor.
  5. Package (1h): thumbnail (face + result + one number), title A/B, chapters, guide-PDF link, ForkScape UTM link, end-screen to the same category's playlist.

Honest Economics

AdSense alone will disappoint you. At education RPMs, even a good outcome — say 50k average views by Phase 2 — is roughly ₹2,000–6,000 per long-form video. The realistic model for this channel is: AdSense (small) + sponsors from ep ~25 (skilling platforms pay well in this niche) + your own funnel (ForkScape downloads, the guide PDFs, and eventually a paid cohort or template pack). The 100-episode library is an asset that compounds through search for years — that long tail, plus the funnel, is the actual business case. Budget emotionally for 6 months of near-zero revenue.

Top Risks, Named

  • Burnout is the #1 killer. Batch-film experiments two at a time; keep 4 episodes of buffer; the phase gates exist so quitting at 30 still leaves a coherent asset.
  • Advice-guru saturation. Never publish an episode where you didn't actually do the thing. The moment it becomes narrated Wikipedia, you're competing with AI slop at AI slop prices.
  • Claim risk. Salary and "growth %" claims in Hindi career content get community-noted and comment-audited. Cite sources on screen; your guide already has them.
  • Product-pushing backlash. Cap ForkScape at its 60–90s segment; the disclosure line every episode; the tool must be shown doing real work.
  • Language economics. Pure Hindi halves CPM; Hinglish delivery with English titles/keywords is the standard fix and matches how the audience actually speaks.
